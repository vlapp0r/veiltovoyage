Best Japan Honeymoon Itinerary: 10 Days of Romance, Ryokans & Ramen

Plan the perfect Japan honeymoon with this 10-day itinerary covering Tokyo, Kyoto, Hakone & more — with tips on ryokans, food & romance.
August 24, 2026 · 8 min · Veil to Voyage

Japan has a way of making you feel like you’ve stepped into a different world entirely — one where ancient temples sit quietly beside neon-lit streets, where a bowl of ramen can move you to silence, and where every detail of your stay feels like it was arranged just for you. For couples, that sense of wonder becomes something genuinely special. Whether you’re sharing a private onsen bath in the mountains or walking hand-in-hand through a bamboo forest at golden hour, Japan has a remarkable talent for making ordinary moments feel extraordinary. If you’ve been searching for the best Japan honeymoon itinerary, you’re in exactly the right place. Let’s map it all out.

Why Japan Is One of the Best Honeymoon Destinations in the World#

Before we get into the day-by-day breakdown, let’s talk about why Japan deserves a spot at the top of your honeymoon shortlist.

  • Incredible variety: In one trip, you can experience a buzzing global megacity, serene mountain hot springs, ancient imperial culture, and coastal fishing villages.
  • Safety and ease of travel: Japan is consistently ranked one of the safest countries in the world, and the public transportation system is famously reliable — a huge stress-reliever when you’re newlyweds navigating an unfamiliar country.
  • World-class hospitality: The Japanese concept of omotenashi (selfless hospitality) means you will be treated with extraordinary care everywhere you go.
  • Food that will genuinely change you: From sushi counters with Michelin stars to late-night izakayas, the food alone is worth the flight.
  • Romantic backdrops at every turn: Cherry blossoms, lantern-lit alleys, misty mountains — Japan is almost unfairly photogenic.

The best time to visit for romance and beauty? March to May for cherry blossoms, or October to November for stunning autumn foliage. Both seasons are magical and mild.

The 10-Day Japan Honeymoon Itinerary at a Glance#

Here’s how we recommend structuring your time across four key destinations:

  • Days 1–3: Tokyo
  • Days 4–5: Hakone
  • Days 6–8: Kyoto
  • Day 9: Nara (day trip)
  • Day 10: Osaka (departure city)

This route flows naturally west along the famous Shinkansen (bullet train) corridor, so you won’t be doubling back or wasting precious honeymoon days in transit. Days 1–3: Tokyo — Dive Into the Energy#

Land at Narita or Haneda, get your JR Pass sorted, and give yourself permission to be completely overwhelmed — in the best way.

Where to stay: Shinjuku or Shibuya for the full city experience, or Asakusa if you want something with a more traditional, old-Tokyo atmosphere.

What to do as a couple:

  • Start with Senso-ji Temple in Asakusa early in the morning before the crowds arrive — it’s peaceful, beautiful, and a perfect first Tokyo memory.
  • Wander the narrow alleys of Yanaka, Tokyo’s best-preserved old neighborhood. It feels like a village tucked inside a metropolis.
  • Splurge on an omakase sushi dinner. You don’t need a Michelin-starred reservation to have a life-changing meal — many excellent counters take bookings online with no fuss.
  • Take an evening stroll through Shinjuku’s Golden Gai — dozens of tiny, intimate bars tucked into an area the size of a city block. Pick one that looks interesting and just walk in.
  • If time allows, spend a morning in Harajuku and Omotesando — equal parts quirky street culture and high-end shopping.

Honeymoon tip: Many Tokyo hotels offer honeymoon packages that include room upgrades, champagne, and late checkout. Always mention your honeymoon when booking — it’s worth asking.

Days 4–5: Hakone — Your Ryokan Moment#

This is the part of the itinerary most couples say they’ll never forget. Hakone is a mountainous hot spring region about 90 minutes from Tokyo by the Romancecar (yes, that’s its actual name — a scenic limited express train that’s as dreamy as it sounds).

The centerpiece of Hakone is Mount Fuji, which presides over the region like a postcard you can’t quite believe is real. On clear days, the reflection of Fuji in Lake Ashi is absolutely stunning.

But the real star here is the ryokan experience.

A ryokan is a traditional Japanese inn where you sleep on futons laid on tatami floors, wear a yukata (cotton robe), and dine on elaborate multi-course kaiseki meals served in your room. Most ryokans feature private or semi-private onsens (hot spring baths), which for couples is nothing short of magical.

What to do:

  • Soak in your onsen — multiple times. That’s the whole point.
  • Take the ropeway over volcanic Owakudani for views of Fuji and the surrounding mountains.
  • Cruise Lake Ashi on a pirate ship (genuinely — it’s touristy and totally worth it).
  • Simply slow down. Hakone is where your honeymoon exhales.

Days 6–8: Kyoto — Ancient Japan at Its Most Romantic#

If Tokyo is Japan’s heartbeat, Kyoto is its soul. The former imperial capital is home to over 1,600 Buddhist temples and 400 Shinto shrines — and somehow, none of it feels like a museum. It all feels very much alive.

Where to stay: The Higashiyama district puts you within walking distance of Kyoto’s most iconic sights and has a beautifully preserved historic streetscape.

What to do as a couple:

  • Walk the Fushimi Inari Shrine at dawn, when the thousands of vermillion torii gates stretch up the mountain in near-perfect silence.
  • Stroll through the Arashiyama Bamboo Grove — yes, it’s busy midday, but go early morning and it’s otherworldly.
  • Spend an afternoon in Gion, Kyoto’s geisha district. The lanes of traditional wooden machiya townhouses are stunning at dusk.
  • Visit Kinkaku-ji (the Golden Pavilion) — cliché for a reason. The gold-leaf temple reflected in its mirror pond is genuinely jaw-dropping.
  • Book a traditional tea ceremony for two. Many studios offer private sessions, and it’s one of the most intimate cultural experiences you can share.

Eat your way through Nishiki Market: This covered food market is narrow, bustling, and packed with vendors selling everything from fresh tofu to pickled vegetables to grilled skewers. Go hungry.

Day 9: Nara — Day Trip for Something Unforgettable#

Just 45 minutes from Kyoto by train, Nara is home to over 1,200 wild sika deer who roam freely through the park and surrounding streets. The deer are considered sacred messengers, and they will absolutely mob you for crackers — which you can buy from vendors throughout the park.

Beyond the deer, Nara is home to Todai-ji Temple, which houses an enormous bronze Buddha and is one of the most architecturally impressive structures in Japan. Give yourself a full morning here, then head back to Kyoto for a final evening stroll through Gion.

Day 10: Osaka — Eat, Explore, Depart#

Osaka is only 15 minutes from Kyoto by Shinkansen, making it an ideal departure city. Even if your flight is in the evening, you have time to:

  • Walk the neon-lit Dotonbori district and eat takoyaki (octopus balls) straight from the street vendor.
  • Visit Osaka Castle and its surrounding park.
  • Grab one last bowl of ramen or a final set of gyoza before you head to Kansai International Airport.

Osaka’s food culture is its own love language — the city’s unofficial motto is kuidaore, meaning “eat until you drop.” Consider it your delicious farewell.

Frequently Asked Questions#

How much should we budget for a 10-day Japan honeymoon? A comfortable but not extravagant budget runs roughly $4,000–$6,000 USD per couple, including flights, accommodation, food, and transport. Ryokan nights will be your biggest expense — expect to pay $200–$500+ per couple per night for a quality experience with meals included. Tokyo and Osaka hotels can be very affordable if you’re flexible on neighborhood.

Do we need to speak Japanese to travel comfortably as honeymooners? Not at all. English signage is widespread in tourist areas, train stations have English displays, and Google Translate’s camera feature handles menus and signs beautifully. Japan is genuinely one of the most foreigner-friendly countries to navigate independently.

Is the JR Pass worth it for this itinerary? For this specific 10-day route — Tokyo, Hakone, Kyoto, Nara, Osaka — a 14-day JR Pass is usually worth it, especially if you’re taking the Shinkansen between cities. Run the numbers against individual ticket prices before you buy, as the math shifted slightly with recent price changes.
