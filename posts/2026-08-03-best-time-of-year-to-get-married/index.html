Best Time of Year to Get Married: A Season-by-Season Breakdown

Discover the best time of year to get married based on weather, budget, and venue availability — plus honest pros and cons for every season.
August 3, 2026 · 8 min · Veil to Voyage

Choosing your wedding date feels a little like choosing a tattoo — permanent, significant, and something you’ll be talking about for the rest of your life. Before you circle a date on the calendar based purely on when your favorite venue had an opening, it’s worth stepping back and thinking through what each season actually looks like on a wedding day. Weather, cost, guest availability, photography lighting, and even flower selection all shift dramatically depending on the time of year. This guide breaks down every season honestly, so you can pick the timing that genuinely fits your vision — not just the one that showed up first on a venue’s availability calendar.

Why Your Wedding Season Matters More Than You Think#

Most couples start with a venue and work backward to a date. That’s not necessarily wrong, but it can lead to surprises — like realizing your dreamy outdoor garden wedding is scheduled right in the middle of peak humidity season, or that you booked a December Saturday without accounting for holiday travel costs for half your guest list.

The season you choose affects:

  • Your budget — peak season means peak pricing for venues, photographers, florists, and caterers
  • Guest attendance — summer and holiday weekends compete with vacations and family obligations
  • Photography quality — golden-hour timing and natural light change significantly by season
  • Floral and décor options — some flowers are simply not available (or cost a fortune) out of season
  • Overall vibe and logistics — from what guests wear to how you plan your timeline

None of these factors should be dealbreakers on their own, but together they paint a pretty clear picture of what you’re signing up for.

Spring Weddings: The Classic Choice#

Spring — roughly March through May — is one of the most beloved wedding seasons, and for good reason. Temperatures are mild in most of the country, flowers are naturally abundant and affordable, and everything is green and blooming without any help from your florist’s budget.

The good stuff:

  • Comfortable temperatures for guests and wedding party alike
  • Stunning natural backdrops without heavy decoration
  • Seasonal flowers like peonies, tulips, ranunculus, and cherry blossoms are at their best
  • Longer daylight hours starting to build through April and May

The honest challenges:

  • Spring weather is unpredictable — rain is a real possibility, especially in March and April
  • April and May book up fast, often a year or more in advance
  • Holiday weekends like Mother’s Day and Easter can conflict with guest travel plans
  • Allergies are no joke — have a plan for outdoor ceremonies if anyone in your party is sensitive

Spring is ideal if you love soft, romantic aesthetics and want a date that feels timeless without the extreme heat or cold. Just have a weather backup plan and book your vendors early.

Summer Weddings: Busy, Beautiful, and Pricey#

June, July, and August make up the traditional peak wedding season in the United States and much of Europe. There’s a reason everyone wants a summer wedding — school is out, days are long, and the idea of a warm outdoor celebration just sounds perfect.

The good stuff:

  • Maximum daylight means more flexible timelines and gorgeous late-evening photos
  • Guests are more available, especially families with kids out of school
  • Outdoor venues are in their prime
  • Destination weddings and honeymoons align perfectly with summer travel season

The honest challenges:

  • This is the most expensive time of year to get married, full stop
  • Venues, photographers, and caterers are often booked 12–18 months out
  • Heat can be brutal — outdoor summer ceremonies in the South or Southwest can be genuinely uncomfortable
  • Guests are also juggling their own vacations, so RSVPs can be unpredictable

If summer is your dream, go for it — just build in extra budget buffer (10–15% more than you think you need) and start planning earlier than feels necessary. Your future self will thank you.

Fall Weddings: The Sweet Spot (That Everyone Is Discovering)#

Ask any wedding photographer what their favorite season to shoot is, and most will say fall without hesitation. September through November offers a combination of incredible light, stunning foliage, and comfortable temperatures that’s hard to beat. It also used to be considered “off-peak,” though that’s less true than it was a decade ago.

The good stuff:

  • Jaw-dropping natural color in most parts of the country
  • Cooler temperatures make for more comfortable guests and a less wilted wedding party
  • Slightly lower pricing than peak summer months — though this gap is closing
  • Rich, warm color palettes feel natural and current
  • Harvest-inspired menus are a caterer’s dream

The honest challenges:

  • Foliage timing is unpredictable and varies by year and region — do your research for your specific location
  • October in particular is now nearly as competitive as summer in many markets
  • Earlier sunsets mean you lose daylight faster, which affects your photo and reception timeline
  • Holiday weekends (Columbus Day, Veterans Day, Thanksgiving) require careful navigation

Fall is genuinely a fantastic choice for couples who want beauty without the full price tag of summer — just know that the secret is largely out, and you’ll still need to book vendors well in advance.

Winter Weddings: Underrated, Intimate, and Often Spectacular#

Winter — December through February — tends to be the least popular wedding season, which is both its biggest challenge and its greatest opportunity. Fewer weddings happening means more vendor availability, lower pricing, and a more intimate feel overall.

The good stuff:

  • Significant cost savings — venues and vendors often offer discounts of 20–30% in off-peak months
  • Easier to book your first-choice vendors without 12+ months of lead time
  • Moody, dramatic aesthetics that photograph beautifully
  • Cozy indoor celebrations with warm lighting feel genuinely magical
  • January and February can be ideal for couples who want a smaller, more intimate gathering

The honest challenges:

  • Weather-related travel disruptions are a real risk, especially for destination guests
  • Outdoor options are limited in most climates
  • December competes directly with holiday travel, parties, and family obligations
  • Some vendors take extended breaks in January and February

One note on holiday proximity: a December wedding close to Christmas or New Year’s can be absolutely stunning, but expect to pay holiday-season venue pricing and accept that some guests may not be able to make the trip. A January or February date sidesteps both issues and keeps more money in your pocket.

How to Choose What’s Right for Your Wedding#

Here’s the honest truth: there’s no universally “best” time of year to get married. There’s only the best time for your priorities. Ask yourself these questions before locking in a date:

  • What’s our budget? If you’re working with a tighter budget, fall and winter offer real savings.
  • What kind of photos do we want? Lush greenery, fall foliage, snowy scenes, or spring blooms — each requires a different season.
  • How important is outdoor space? If your dream requires an outdoor ceremony, summer and fall are your safest bets.
  • Who absolutely needs to be there? Think through major conflicts — graduations, holidays, school calendars — before committing.
  • Where are we honeymooning? Some destinations have ideal travel windows that may influence your date.

Once you’ve answered these, pull up your ideal venue’s availability and let the intersection of your priorities and their calendar guide you. That’s usually where the right date lives.

Frequently Asked Questions#

What is the most popular month to get married? October and June consistently rank as the top two wedding months in the United States. Both offer beautiful weather in most regions, though this popularity also means higher demand and pricing. If you have your heart set on either month, plan to book vendors at least 12–18 months in advance.

What is the cheapest time of year to get married? January and February are generally the most affordable months for weddings, followed by November and early December (excluding the week of Christmas). You can often save 20–30% on venue and vendor costs compared to peak summer months, and you’ll have far more flexibility in scheduling.

Is it bad luck to get married in certain months? There’s an old English rhyme that warns against marrying in May (“Marry in May, rue the day”), but this is purely superstition with no real-world basis. Choose your date based on logistics, budget, and what feels right for you — not folklore.

How far in advance should we book our wedding date? For peak season (June through October), 12–18 months is a safe planning window, especially in popular markets or for sought-after venues. For off-peak dates in winter months, 6–12 months is often sufficient, though it’s still wise to lock in your venue and photographer as early as possible since those two vendors tend to book first.

Picking your wedding date is one of the first big decisions you’ll make together as an engaged couple, and it sets the tone for everything that follows. Whatever season calls to you — whether it’s May peonies, October foliage, or a cozy February evening — the most important thing is that the date feels yours.
